Soft Robotics · Control
Mar 2026 – May 2026 · Prof. Madhu Vadali, IITGN

Modeling and Control of a Variable-Stiffness Soft Robotic Gripper

A soft gripper with variable stiffness through combined pneumatic and tendon actuation — modeled from first principles and regulated with an LQR controller and state observer.

MATLABPRB ModelingState-SpaceLQRObserver Design
Project Report
Project details

  1. Nonlinear modeling

    Modeled the variable-stiffness gripper using a nonlinear Pseudo-Rigid-Body (PRB) model capturing pneumatic and tendon actuation.

  2. Linearization

    Derived a linearized state-space representation of the system about its operating point.

  3. System analysis

    Analysed the linearized model to inform controller design.

  4. Controller & observer design

    Designed an LQR controller and a Luenberger observer in MATLAB for position and force regulation.

  5. Closed-loop validation

    Validated performance through closed-loop simulations with external disturbances and sensor noise.

Key Innovation

Combining pneumatic and tendon actuation for variable stiffness, captured in a PRB model that is tractable for model-based control.

Methodology

Nonlinear PRB modeling → linearized state-space model → LQR state feedback with a Luenberger observer, all in MATLAB.

Key Findings

The closed-loop system achieved robust position and force regulation under disturbances and sensor noise.

Applications & Impact

Soft grippers with tunable stiffness suit handling of delicate or irregular objects, where both compliance and grip force matter.

IC Engines · Emissions
Dec 2023 – Apr 2024 · Prof. K. Kishor, CBIT · B.Tech Project

Reducing Emissions in a Single-Cylinder Diesel Engine with Cottonseed-Oil Blends and an Unconventional Catalytic Converter

Optimised diesel–cottonseed oil blends and built a low-cost catalytic converter, improving efficiency while sharply cutting emissions.

BiofuelsIC EnginesCatalytic ConverterFabrication
Paper (IJESE)
Project details
58.8%Lower CO emissions
95.5%Lower smoke (HSU)
+8.7%Brake thermal efficiency
+8%Mileage

  1. Blend optimisation

    Designed and ran a study to find the optimal blend of cottonseed oil and diesel fuel.

  2. Catalytic converter design

    Designed a cost-effective catalytic converter using cerium oxide, charcoal and sponge iron catalysts.

  3. Fabrication

    Fabricated the converter and integrated it with a single-cylinder diesel engine test setup.

  4. Performance & emission testing

    Measured mileage, brake thermal efficiency, air–fuel ratio, CO, HC and smoke levels.

Key Innovation

An unconventional, low-cost catalytic converter built from cerium oxide, charcoal and sponge iron instead of expensive precious-metal catalysts.

Methodology

Experimental blend testing on a single-cylinder diesel engine, followed by emission testing with and without the fabricated converter.

Key Findings

  • +8% mileage, +8.7% brake thermal efficiency, +6.54% air–fuel ratio
  • 58.8% less CO, 15.21% less HC
  • 95.5% reduction in Hartridge Smoke Units (HSU)

Applications & Impact

A low-cost route to cleaner diesel engines using renewable biofuel blends and affordable catalyst materials.
